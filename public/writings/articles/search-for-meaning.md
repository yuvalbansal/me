## History Of Curiosity

Throughout the course of history, humans have been asking various questions about the physical world and life. From Thales attempting to explain the origin of the universe in sixth century BCE or the Ancient Indian philosophies concerned with the nature of reality and the self even before Thales, humans have been thinking beings since time immemorial. All the scientific discoveries of today can be attributed to this inquisitive nature of our species. No other life form seems to possess higher self-awareness than us.

Many theories about the physical world have been debunked long ago in the past. For a man living in the Iron Age, the Earth was flat with all the celestial bodies revolving around the Earth under the influence of a God, who rewards and punishes everyone as he wills. Some of these celestial bodies were viewed as Gods themselves and the natural calamities were considered to be the wrath of God, until recently.

Every once in a while, there came people who challenged the existing beliefs of the society. Most of them were turned into social outcasts and some of them like Socrates and Jesus of Nazareth had to face fatal consequences. Some notable figures whose ideas shaped modern human thought will include Siddhartha Gautama, Confucius, Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, Jesus Christ, Thomas Aquinas, René Descartes, John Locke, Immanuel Kant, Johann Wolfgang von Goethe and Karl Marx among many others.

The deeper and more philosophical questions are often left unanswered even after exercising our reason and perception to the best of our abilities. At such moments, it may seem logical to believe in a supernatural being that might be the cause of everything.

## The One

Around 4000 years ago, somewhere in the Middle East, God made a covenant with Abraham (also known as Ibrahim). Abraham was commanded to leave his homeland and go to a place that He would show him. In return, he would receive a great nation, his name would be great, his lineage and everyone who honoured him would be blessed, everyone who dishonoured him would be cursed, and he will be used to bless everyone on earth. However, breaking this covenant would result in a punishment of death, when this agreement was to be made between Abraham and God, God caused a deep sleep to fall over Abraham and took it all on himself alone. He declared, "If this covenant fails (even if your descendants break it) let the punishment fall on Me." Thus, God instructed circumcision on Abraham and his descendants, and it became a unilateral oath by God. For centuries, humanity repeatedly failed to uphold their faithfulness and ethics. Yet, the legal punishment fell on Him. Hence, He reincarnated as Jesus to be crucified for the redemption of humanity from the debt of the ancient curse.

It was through this unified lineage, the three major Abrahamic religions, namely Judaism, Christianity and Islam, were introduced through different founders or prophets in the forementioned chronological order. They are strictly monotheistic, as they believe in one supreme God. God created this universe (including light, sky, land, plants and vegetation, sun, moon, stars, all animals and the first humans) out of nothing in six days, around 6000 years ago. Hence, God is viewed as personal, omnipotent (all-powerful), omniscient (all-knowing) being, deeply involved in human history. These religions strictly follow their respective sacred texts, the Torah, Bible or Quran, as the "word of God" because the scripture says that it is the "word of God".

Although personifying or visually depicting God is generally considered disrespectful in these religions, I find their idea of God to be very human-like. While theology states God as infinite and beyond material world, thus making him unrepresentable in the real world, the narrative texts of the Abrahamic religions frequently describe God using human behaviours, emotional states, and relational dynamics. The scriptures often depict God experiencing human-like emotions like wrath, jealousy, grief and compassion. God is also known to interact through humanity in human social frameworks like making legal contracts (or covenants), negotiations, and later changing its mind, testing loyalty and favouritism.

Since then, people from the three of these religions have been praying to this same God for His mercy. Nevertheless, there have been historic and ongoing tensions among the three Abrahamic religions which stems from a theological paradox. They worship the same God, revere the same prophets, yet their core doctrines are mutually exclusive. All three religions claim to know the final truth and claim the exact same piece of land in Jerusalem as theirs. Thus, brutal conflicts have led to bloodshed in numerous wars and internal chaos spanning over centuries between the followers of these religions.

## Neoplatonism

Another school of philosophy originated in third century CE have widely influenced the Christian, Jewish and Islamic philosophies. Primarily based on the ideas of Plato, it was founded by another Greek philosopher Plotinus. It is a belief that all reality is derived from a single source called "The One". The universe is divided in various hierarchies namely the One (or the Good), the intellect (or Nous), the soul and the material world. The One allows each of the other three aspects to come into existence in the hierarchical order.

The One is the absolute first principle from where the reality is derived. The intellect is the divine mind and the world of Platonic forms (the eternal universal concepts of all things). The soul is the bridge between the intellect and the material world. The material world is the lowest form of reality consisting of the physical world, space and time. Similar to darkness, which is the absence of light, all the evil and negativity is considered to be the absence of the One. The ultimate goal is to ascend the soul and reunite it with the One.

The idea of the One resonates with the monotheistic view of Abrahamic religions. Parallels were drawn between the Neoplatonic propositions and the Abrahamic God, upon whose will the universe came into existence from nothing. In spite of their belief in the eternal truth laid out by their respective sacred texts, these religions have evolved and adapted over the years through re-interpretation of the same texts to abandon old practices and elevate their ethical stance over issues like slavery, patriarchy and religious intolerance.

## The Many

I was born as a Hindu myself. Often considered as the oldest surviving religion still practiced by over a billion people, Hinduism has evolved as a mix of traditions and beliefs without any founder attributed to it. It is so old that nobody actually knows how old it is. It has always existed and evolved in multiple forms simultaneously. Hinduism has based its philosophies on the ideas of various philosophers and sages (or rishis) alongside old texts ranging from the Vedas, Upanishads, Puranas, Dharmashastras and the popular epics (classified as itihasa or history) like Ramayana and Mahabharata.

The relationship between ordinary Hindus and the classical scriptures have changed significantly over time. The contemporary form of the religion has shifted from being centred on the scriptures and inquiry to devotion and traditions. While most texts were never intended for everyone as they were orally preserved by the priestly communities, relatively fewer people today have studied them in depth. There is a stark difference between the scholarly teachings and popular practices.

Hindu view is often described as polytheistic and millions of Hindus believe in many gods and goddesses. While there are numerous deities associated with different aspects of life and the universe, the major deities include Brahma (the creator), Vishnu (the preserver and protector) and Shiva (the transformer and destroyer). A unique feature of Hindu worship is the idea of choosing a personal deity. A person is free to worship any deity in their own way through puja, mantra, fasting, meditation or service. However, it is often considered as different expressions of the same ultimate reality called Brahman. From a philosophical perspective, Hinduism can be viewed as both monistic and monotheistic.

Hinduism allows for self reflection and no one is entitled to believe in the same scriptures or schools of thought as others. Historically, some branches of Hinduism have even shared beliefs like atheism, agnosticism and materialism. Some philosophies reject the Vedas outright (nastika), while some internalize it word for word (astika). Some philosophies based their beliefs on empirical knowledge alone (vaiseshika), while some were dualists classifying reality into purusha and prakriti (samkhya). Some beliefs were similar to the modern existentialism (charvaka), while some believed in discipline (yoga).

According to Hinduism, the universe repeats a cycle of creation, preservation, and destruction spanning durations of 311.04 trillion human years (around 100 Brahma years which is the lifespan of Brahma). Current universe is roughly halfway through the cycle making the universe (first day of the 51st year of Brahma) around 155 trillion human years old. Humans are created at every dawn of Brahma which was around 1.97 billion human years ago, for the current Brahma day (or Kalpa).

I find many ideas from ancient Hindu philosophy to be deeply intriguing. We have come a long way and, ironically, the modern practice is filled with religious dogma, superstitions and hypocrisy.

## The None

Another major religion and philosophy emerged in the 6th to 5th century BCE in India is called Buddhism. With around half a billion followers today, it was founded by a seeker Siddhartha Gautama popularly known as the Buddha. It can be classified as a non-theistic religion which denies the existence of an all-powerful creator.

As the story goes, Siddhartha Gautam was born as a prince in ancient India. He was surrounded with wealth, palaces and pleasures all his early life. At the age of 29, Siddhartha went on a journey outside his palace walls, where he witnessed "The Four Sights" that had a profound impact on him. He encountered an old man (showing him that ageing is inevitable), a sick man (revealing that disease cause suffering), a dead body (making him realise that death is a reality for all) and a peaceful holy man. Driven by a fierce desire to seek the truth about human suffering, he renounced his royal luxurious life, left his kingdom, his wife and his child, traded his royal garments with simple robes, and became a wanderer.

He studied under various spiritual teachers for six years and pushed his body to extreme limits by severe fasting and meditation, nearly starving to death. This experience made him realise that extreme conditions only harmed his body and mind without bringing him closer to the ultimate truth. Eventually, he gave up wandering and sat in deep meditation under a sacred tree (now known as the Bodhi Tree). He vowed not to move until he found the answer to human suffering. At the age of 35, he achieved enlightenment.

This breakthrough occurred in three phases of a night. In the evening, he gained knowledge of his past lives and cycles of rebirth. By midnight, he understood the law of karma and the cycles of the universe. Till dawn, he realised the Four Noble Truths and dependent origination, achieving the ultimate state of "Nirvana".

The Four Noble Truths are:

- Dukkha (The Illness) - Inescapable suffering, unsatisfactoriness, and instability
- Samudaya (The Cause) - Cravings, attachments and ignorance
- Nirodha (The Cure) - Suffering ends when craving and attachment are completely extinguished (Nirvana)
- Magga (The Treatment) - The Eightfold Path to end suffering (wisdom, ethical conduct and mental discipline)

The Buddha spent the next 45 years of his life teaching anyone who would listen. At the age of 80, he passed away. His final words are said to be: "All conditioned things are impermanent. Work out your own salvation with diligence."

## Mysticism

Humanity have been following religions at all times in human history. Ever since, they have been stressing over to become the most devout followers of their respective religions. Over thousands of years, there have been intellectuals like Galileo Galilei, Voltaire and Charles Darwin who have struggled living with their contemporary religions, often enraging the religious communities. There have been claims about the nature of reality to be monistic (Plotinus, Spinoza), dualistic (Plato, Descartes) or pluralistic (Empedocles, Anaxagoras). Across all civilizations, while others were busy interpreting the scriptures and performing rituals, there were the mystics who experienced inner peace and joy by complete surrender of the worldly desires.

Mysticism is the pursuit of direct experiences of the ultimate reality or the divine. They seek to experience the deepest form of existence, not merely through belief, rituals or intellectual understanding, but through transformation. For instance, a person can read a thousand books about oceans, but it is different from actually swimming in it. Mystics have existed with diverse beliefs about God. While some had unwavering faith in one god (either omnipotent or deistic), others believed in the existence of multiple deities. Some mystics even had non-theistic or naturalistic understanding of the universe.

There is no fixed set of mystic beliefs as it has existed in various forms. Hindus had Yog and Bhakti Mysticism, Buddhists had Zen, Christians had Hesychasm, Muslims had Sufism, and Jews had Kabbalah. Other forms of Mysticism include Taoism from China which focuses on living in harmony with the "ultimate source" of the universe, and Pythagoreanism as founded by the Greek mathematician Pythagoras who taught the reality to be mathematical and appealed his followers to study maths and geometry to purify their soul. Some mystic practices like meditation, contemplation, singing, serving others and self-discipline are followed widely across beliefs.

Many people, who were not mystics in a formal sense, have claimed to experience mystical states at some point in their lifetimes. Joan of Arc, Rumi, Albert Einstein, Carl Jung and Friedrich Nietzsche (a publicly known atheist) claim to have had such experiences. Neurobiological research shows that due to extreme stress, near-death trauma, or deep contemplation, the brain can lose its sense of "I", creating a sudden feeling of absolute oneness with the world.

I feel drawn towards Mysticism as a way of life. I conformed with Buddha's philosophy when he stated that our desires and attachments are the cause of our suffering. A mystic doesn't bother from material gains or losses in order to live his life with satisfaction, as he dedicates his life for a singular purpose.

## The Myths

Humans have been observing the universe ever since we got the ability to think. Charles Darwin sailed around the world from 1831 to 1836 collecting plant, animal and rock samples, and spent decades analysing specimens. He noticed that the fossils of the extinct species resembled the living species in the same regions. Also, the mockingbirds, flinches and tortoises had distinct physical differences based on the island they lived on. Additionally, he studied how farmers bred animals and plants to get desired traits. An essay by Thomas Malthus explained how "Population growth outpaces food supply, creating competition" which had a profound impact on his research. After looking out for the patterns in his observations, he proposed the theory of evolution by natural selection which was consistent with what he had experienced. The scientific community widely accepted his theory as a logical mechanism and considered his observations as evidence of his claims.

Similarly, many religious beliefs are often based on observations and (however flawed it may be) reasonings. In the Abrahamic religions, Earth was believed to be the centre of the universe as it appeared to have occupied a special place and other celestial bodies seemed to be moving around it. They also believed that the sky is solid and like a dome in shape because it appeared as a dome with rainwater coming from above the heavens. According to Hindu belief, the solar and lunar eclipses were viewed as inauspicious or negative periods. They lacked scientific explanations, hence, interpreted the sudden darkness to be caused by supernatural causes like a demon temporarily swallowing the Sun or the Moon. For someone who is observing without any prior knowledge on the subject, these theories can sound reasonable. However, today, we neither classify these theories as logical mechanisms nor do we look at the observations as evidences for their claims because we have found more reasonable explanations for those phenomenons.

Science has progressed by constantly replacing the pre-existing beliefs with new more logical beliefs. Meanwhile, the religions were busy disputing over the centuries old concepts. Yet, a modern day Christian would not claim the Earth to be flat and the universe to be geocentric. Ironically, even the devout followers of religions often drop few handpicked bogus beliefs in order to fit in the existing society.

We have always searched for patterns and meaning in everything we see. Often, these patterns are just coincidences instead of an underlying phenomenon. That is why even after contemplation for over millennia, there are plenty of pseudoscience and myths like homeopathy, astrology, numerology and tarot that we still believe in. Many people have claimed these false beliefs to have worked for them (coincidently), which reinforces the belief of the larger community in such myths.

## History Of The Universe

Historically, there have been multiple theories about the formation of this universe. Most of these theories were based on various assumptions or were deeply flawed. In 1927, a Belgian astronomer and Catholic priest Georges Lemaître first proposed the famous Big Bang Theory. After decades of extensive research, we can say with absolute certainty that the universe is 13.8 billion years old. Thanks to the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), Hubble's Law of expansion, and element measurement, we know the history of the universe with high precision.

The shortest fundamental unit of time in physics is approximately 5.4e-44 seconds (also known as Planck time). It marks the threshold where our current physical models breakdown. At the first Planck time of the big bang (also known as the Planck Epoch) around 13.8 billion years ago, the portion of universe that we see today was about the size of a Planck length (~ 1.6e-35 meters), though the entire universe as a whole may be much larger or even infinite. Science knows nothing concrete about the universe before Planck time except that it was super blazingly hot. The universe expanded faster than the speed of light during a period called cosmic inflation, lasting roughly from 1e-37 to 1e-35 seconds after the big bang.

Around after 1 microsecond of the big bang, the first particles (neutrons, protons and electrons) formed. The first hydrogen and helium nuclei formed 3 minutes after big bang. At 380,000 years after the big bang, the universe cooled enough for the nuclei to be able to capture electrons, called the epoch of recombination. As the electrons were now bound into atoms, cosmic fog cleared and the universe became transparent for light to travel over great distances. The formation of these atoms through electron capture produced light. This glow is still detectable today, which is called the cosmic microwave background. Stars formed 200 million years later and galaxies after another 200 million years. The universe has been expanding ever since. According to the latest studies, cosmic expansion is speeding up. While we are not sure yet, but it is likely that the universe will continue to expand forever.

We know the history of the universe with high precision after 380,000 year mark through direct observation of light. The Sun and Earth formed about 4.6 billion years and 4.54 billion ago respectively. Around 3.5 to 4.1 billion years ago, first simple-single celled living organisms emerged on our planet. Owing to evolution, it took billions of years for first true animals to appear on Earth. It was not until 500 million years ago that we had diverse animal life, especially in the waters. Dinosaurs appeared around 252 million years ago, ruling the planet for millions of years, before getting extincted by a massive asteroid hit 66 million years ago.

The modern humans (Homo sapiens) evolved in Africa approximately 300,000 years ago. Scientists presume that the humans developed the foundations of self-awareness and symbolic thinking about 100,000 years ago as they underwent physical changes in the brain and started developing cultural artifacts. Early humans lived as nomadic gatherers and started settling down around 10,000 years ago to live in permanent homes. Traces from early rituals, cave art and ancient idols from tens of thousands of years suggest that humans have evolved their spiritual beliefs ever since they gained consciousness. While religions had existed long before writing began, we find recorded history of organised religions as early as 5,200 years ago in Mesopotamia, with the invention of writing.

## Why God Is Dead

Some humans philosophized and some deceived others which influenced the masses and eventually formed religions. From then onwards, the successive humans used the pre-existing religions to fulfill their current philosophical, ethical and emotional needs. On the onset of modern scientific discoveries in the nineteenth and the twentieth centuries, a sharp transformation occurred in human societies. The developing scientific rationality had eroded the societal belief in God and the religious worldview. Humans no longer needed religion and the idea of God to answer the physical questions. We gained precise understanding of the motion of celestial objects and realised how our galaxy is just one of billions in an expanding universe, which reduced Earth to a microscopic speck. The theory of evolution by natural selection demonstrated how humans developed over millions of years from common ancestors shared with the great apes. We could suddenly explain many natural phenomenons as well human behaviours through the study of geoscience and psychology.

The theists would find it arrogant but brave of the atheists to claim the inexistence of god. But only a true atheist will remain unaffected to such a belief as it would occur to him more naturally.

This brings up an entirely novel problem. As humans begin to discard religions and God, the strong foundation of their morality and purpose breaks apart. Nietzsche referred to this as a crisis and declared in his 1882 book, The Gay Science, that "God is dead".

In this regard, the French philosopher Jean-Paul Sartre popularized Existentialism and proposed his theory of existentialism in the 1940s. Sartre's theory of existentialism states that "existence precedes essence", making humans completely free and responsible for their own choices. Therefore, they must create their own meaning in life without following any pre-defined model. Instead of perceiving it as a crisis, he appealed for radically taking up the responsibility. There is no absolute truth to conform to. As there is no God, there is no fixed blueprint of human life, and they are "condemned to be free" and to choose their meaning.

Humans have always looked out at the boundless cosmos with hope to find a meaning, only to receive eternal silence in return. Another French philosopher Albert Camus, proposed a different approach called Absurdism to tackle this problem. While Existentialists propose to invent our own meaning, Absurdists would argue to accept and stop searching for any meaning at all. They would urge us to face the silence of the universe, and rebel by enjoying life anyway. It is a trap to try to invent a meaning. True freedom will come from laughing at the futility of it all, living with passion.

## Love and Passion

In my opinion, there is always a risk of slipping into existential crisis for an Absurdist. While an Existentialist binds himself to a purpose, an Absurdist holds the enduring belief of life to be meaningless, similar to a nihilist. However, as soon as it seems to me that I have gained some clarity and control over life, I come across people who shake my understanding to its core.

In 1953, only weeks after earning his medical degree, the Argentine physician Che Guevara turned away from a promising career as a medical practitioner to lead to a life of political activism. Eventually, he solidified his decision to abandon medicine for armed revolution. The next six years defined his ideology and he achieved recognition at a global stage. He served as the Marxist military commander for Fidel Castro's army and led various groups of armed guerrillas to execute the strategically planned revolution. Till 1959, Che had brought the Cuban Revolution overthrowing the US-backed dictatorship in Cuba. In 1965, he voluntarily resigned from his position as the top government minister in Cuba, and left his wife and children behind to fight in foreign jungles. He fought against imperialism and capitalism across Argentina, Guatemala, Mexico, Cuba, Congo, and Bolivia through violent revolution before getting captured and shot by the Bolivian army with assistance from the CIA in 1967 at the age of 39.

Nelson Mandela spent 27 years in jail fighting against apartheid in South Africa. Bhagat Singh faced execution at age 23 for his bold defiance against British rule in India. Being an Absurdist even when I know that people like Nelson Mandela, Bhagat Singh and Che Guevara have walked on earth, feels like belittling their lives.

Many artists were known to have dedicated their lives to their art. There are legendary figures like the painter Vincent Van Gogh, poet and novelist Sylvia Plath, and musician Kurt Cobain who lived a deeply troubled life before getting consumed by their art in their 20s and 30s.

For Existentialists, the purpose of living is often connected to people. We may choose to live for our children, parents, partner or other people that are affected by us. Also, there are countless stories of people who dedicated their whole lives to love. Human love can become a path towards divine madness. I find it ridiculous and honourable at the same time to live a life that loses its social identity, ambition and worldly desires due to love. It is something that is difficult to express in words without seeming unreasonable, for love can leave someone in an altered state of existence. For someone who truly believes in the idea of the absurd, bliss is supposed to be eternal without the requirement of a source, but love may still act as a source of bliss for him.

Reading about such personalities leaves me dumbfounded, as I attempt to relate to the passion of these people for the betterment of society, art or love. I cannot resist myself from drawing parallels between the lives of these passionate individuals and the mystics. While it takes immense courage, I find that having a strong singular purpose is one of noblest forms of living.
